# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Two consumer audiences that matter equally. First, patients in Cameroon seeking everyday care — consultations with generalists and specialists, lab work, and affordable coverage — often on modest budgets and variable connectivity. Second, the Cameroonian diaspora abroad, arranging and funding healthcare for family back home and wanting proof that money reaches real, trustworthy care. Both arrive anxious about cost, legitimacy, and whether the service actually works for their situation. Healthcare providers and institutions (doctors, labs, partners) are a secondary audience, routed to a separate partner path.

## Product Purpose

SOSAN is a health-access platform for Africa, delivered through a mobile app and a supporting web presence. It connects people to trusted medical care (telemedicine and in-person appointments), transparent and traceable payments, personalized support, and affordable health-insurance plans — with dedicated products for the diaspora to cover relatives. This site's job is to convert visitors into app installs while making SOSAN feel legitimate and safe enough to trust with health, money, and family. Success is measured primarily in App Store / Play Store downloads driven from the site.

## Positioning

The single platform that connects patients and their diaspora families to trusted, transparent healthcare across Cameroon — consult, pay clearly, and protect family, from anywhere. Every screen reinforces that reaching real care is now possible without the usual friction, opacity, or distance.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Download the SOSAN app (App Store / Play Store).
- Secondary CTA: Explore the Diaspo insurance plans (Silver / Gold / Premium) — the second door for visitors not ready to install, especially diaspora comparing coverage. Lower-commitment entry points (WhatsApp AI assistant, contact form) support but do not replace these two.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Reliable, affordable healthcare for everyone — from your phone, wherever you are."
- Belief ladder (in order, before the primary CTA): (1) This is legitimate and safe — backed by real institutions. (2) It solves a problem I actually have — reaching doctors, avoiding overbilling, covering family. (3) It works for my situation specifically — in Cameroon or abroad in the diaspora. (4) Payment and coverage are transparent — traceable, no hidden fees. (5) Getting started is easy — download the app, or explore Diaspo plans first.
- Proof on hand: Partner and institutional logos are real and carry the legitimacy load — MINSANTE (Ministry of Health), Activa (insurance), Baker Tilly, Afrique Future, SBA, UCAC — plus live presence on both app stores. The three on-page testimonials currently use placeholder names (Wade Warren, Jacob Jones, Leslie Alexander) and should be treated as illustrative until real, attributed quotes replace them; they must not be presented as verified proof.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, warm, and accessible. The voice is a dependable health partner families rely on — reassuring and human, never clinical-cold and never hype-driven. It speaks plainly to people worried about cost and legitimacy, leads with proof and transparency, and treats the patient at home and the relative abroad as equally welcome in the same breath. The dominant feeling on arrival is trust and reassurance: safe, legitimate, dependable.

## Anti-references

Explicitly avoid all four of these: a cold, corporate hospital or insurance site (sterile stock imagery, bland institutional voice); a flashy startup / crypto aesthetic (neon gradients, "disrupt healthcare" hype); a cluttered, ad-heavy portal or classifieds look (dense, banner-stuffed, noisy); and the generic SaaS gradient template that could belong to any product. None of these earn trust with this audience.

## Design Principles

Trust is the product — every screen must earn belief (legitimacy, transparency, proof) before it asks for the install; the pitch never precedes the reassurance.

Two homes, one welcome — address the patient in Cameroon and the relative abroad in the same breath; neither is a footnote, and the diaspora path is first-class, not an add-on.

Warm, not clinical; clear, not flashy — carry human warmth without hospital sterility and confidence without startup hype, which is what all four anti-references have in common.

Transparency as a feature — make "traceable payments, no hidden fees, clear coverage tiers" concrete and visible on the page, not a slogan; it is the core differentiator, so show it.

Lead to the app, but give the unready a real second door — the download is the destination, yet visitors not ready to install must find a genuine next step (Diaspo plans, WhatsApp assistant) rather than a dead end.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA: sufficient color contrast for body and placeholder text, visible keyboard focus states, and semantic markup throughout. The audience skews toward variable connectivity and a wide device range, so keep the experience fast and legible on lower-end phones. Reduced-motion handling is already implemented in CSS (`prefers-reduced-motion`), neutralizing the decorative loops and entrance animations.
