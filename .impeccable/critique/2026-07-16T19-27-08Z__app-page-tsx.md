## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? Mostly no — but with two visible tells.**

**LLM assessment:** This is above the slop line. It commits to a real two-color identity (Vital Green + Trust Navy), uses tinted (not pure-black) shadows, custom stroke icons, an asymmetric services row instead of an identical 4-card grid, a genuinely distinctive WhatsApp-mascot banner, and real partner logos. That is craft, not a template. The two tells that remain: (1) a tracked uppercase **eyebrow on every section** (QUI SOMMES-NOUS / DIASPO SUPPORT / POURQUOI NOUS REJOINDRE / AVIS & COMMENTAIRES) — the AI-grammar scaffold DESIGN.md itself names as "seasoning, not scaffolding"; and (2) **numbered benefit cards (1–4)** in the provider section, where the numbers carry no real sequence.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` on `app/page.tsx` → 2 advisory findings, both the same undocumented color **#F5A623** (amber) at lines 346 and 369 — the Gold/Premium plan dots and, in CSS, the testimonial star rating. Real palette drift: an accent color used three times that never made it into DESIGN.md. Not a false positive; either document it as a tier/rating role or replace it.

**Visual overlays:** No user-visible overlay was injected (single-context run, no live-server overlay step). Findings are grounded in fresh desktop + mobile full-page screenshots and measured contrast values, not an in-page overlay.

## Overall Impression

A trustworthy, well-built health landing page that is **selling before it has earned the sale**. The craft is here — the problem is order and proof. Your #1 strategic principle is "Trust is the product: earn belief before the ask," yet the hero drops App Store / Play Store buttons on the visitor before a single trust signal appears, and the real proof (institutional partners, testimonials) is stranded 8–9 sections down. The single biggest opportunity: **hoist proof upward and fix the green-text contrast**, so the page walks the belief ladder it was designed around.

## What's Working

- **A committed, legible identity.** Vital Green + Trust Navy on a true-white ground (not the cream/sand AI default) reads as care + authority. The hero, the mascot banner, and the navy provider section give the page real rhythm and personality.
- **The WhatsApp-assistant banner.** The cut-out mascot over the green gradient is the most memorable, least-templated block on the page — distinctive without being gimmicky, and it makes the "talk before you install" path feel human.
- **Structure and responsiveness are sound.** Clear section cadence, an asymmetric services row that dodges the identical-grid cliché, a properly highlighted middle pricing tier, and a mobile layout that stacks cleanly with a real hamburger overlay.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The belief ladder is inverted — the page asks for the install before it earns trust.**
- **Why it matters:** Your primary goal is app downloads and your stated audience arrives *anxious about legitimacy* (health + money + family). But the hero's store buttons are the first ask, and the proof that would justify it — MINSANTE, Activa, UCAC, Baker Tilly logos, plus testimonials — doesn't appear until the bottom third. Visitors decide trust in seconds; the evidence arrives too late to inform the decision.
- **Fix:** Add a slim trust bar directly under the hero — "En partenariat avec" + the institutional logos (they're already assets) — and/or a one-line proof cue near the store buttons. Pull the partners marquee up above the fold's fold. Let proof precede the pitch.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

**[P1] Vital Green as text fails WCAG contrast (2.74:1 on white).**
- **Why it matters:** #0fb36a on white measures 2.74:1 — below 4.5:1 for normal text *and* below 3:1 for large text. It's used for **every section eyebrow (13px), every service-card title (18px bold), and the plan prices.** This directly violates your WCAG AA target and DESIGN.md's own "keep green off reading surfaces" rule. Low-vision users and anyone in Cameroon sunlight will struggle to read your section headings.
- **Fix:** Use **green-dark #0a8f54 (4.14:1)** or Ink for any green *text*; reserve #0fb36a for fills, icons, dots, and accents — exactly what the Vital-Green Restraint Rule already prescribes.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable colorize`

**[P1] The testimonials read as fabricated — and fabricated proof on a health brand backfires.**
- **Why it matters:** "Wade Warren", "Jacob Jones", "Leslie Alexander" are recognizable Figma placeholder names, paired with five stars and generic quotes. On a service asking families to trust it with health and money, a testimonial block that looks invented does more damage than no testimonials at all — the stress-tester and the diaspora funder will both clock it.
- **Fix:** Replace with real, attributed quotes (name, city, role), or reframe explicitly as illustrative, or remove the section until real proof exists. Localize names to the audience (Cameroonian/diaspora).
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Missing legal + trust infrastructure for a health/payments service.**
- **Why it matters:** Privacy Policy and Terms are disabled dead placeholders; a service handling health data and payments is exactly the kind that visitors (and app-store review) expect to see them. The "FR ▾" language control and the footer social icons are also non-functional — small, but each dead affordance chips at credibility.
- **Fix:** Ship real Privacy/Terms (even minimal), wire or remove the language toggle, and either link real social accounts or drop the icons.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

**[P2] Eyebrow scaffolding on every section — an AI tell, in a failing color.**
- **Why it matters:** Four sections each open with a tracked uppercase green kicker. That's the exact pattern your own DESIGN.md flags as "seasoning, not scaffolding," and it compounds P1 because it's rendered in the failing green. It makes the page feel more machine-generated than it needs to.
- **Fix:** Keep one or two eyebrows where they genuinely name a theme; drop the rest and let the headline carry the section. Recolor survivors to green-dark or ink.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer):** The Diaspo tiers describe coverage ("Couverture 80% des soins courants") but show **no price** — a first-timer can't tell what any plan costs or how to choose. The page also presents four competing CTAs (download, WhatsApp, contact, "Choisir [plan]") with no single obvious first step.

**Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester):** Will immediately flag the placeholder testimonial names, the dead footer links (privacy/terms/social), the non-functional "FR ▾", and the copy collision where **"80%" appears for both the Silver and Gold tiers** — reads as unfinished.

Red flags: proof (partner institutions) is buried far below; testimonials look fake; the Diaspo plans give no cost and no "how it works for someone abroad" steps; the reassuring specifics she'd want (EVASAN, medical ambulances Yaoundé/Douala) are hidden inside Premium card prose rather than surfaced as diaspora-facing proof.

## Minor Observations

- **Undocumented amber #F5A623** (detector, ×2): used for Gold/Premium dots and testimonial stars. Add it to DESIGN.md as a tier/rating role, or replace it — right now it's palette drift. Note the star color at 2.03:1 conveys rating by color alone.
- **"80%" reused** across Silver and Gold tier descriptions reads copy-pasted; differentiate the numbers.
- **Muted Faint #7a8896 (3.63:1)** on tiny stat labels and testimonial roles is under 4.5:1 — small but real; nudge toward Muted-Soft.
- **No active-section indication** in the nav as you scroll; cheap way to add "you are here."
- **Peak-end:** the page ends on a form + footer. Consider closing on a reassurance/proof beat so the last impression reinforces trust.
